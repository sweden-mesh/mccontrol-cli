Sending a command

"c" asks for a command and sends it to the tagged devices, or to the
cursor device when nothing is tagged; the overlay says which. Up recalls
earlier commands; Enter sends. Each device gets its own signed publish;
each answer lands in the results tile as it comes back, and a note says
how many answered.

The command line at the bottom of the results tile does the same without
an overlay: Tab to it (or click it), type, Enter. It names its target
before the arrow ("Gisebo ▶", "2 tagged ▶"); the replies land above it as
they arrive, and a command it cannot send -- one the node would refuse,
a caution answered with anything but "y", tags in two regions -- is said
on a line of its own there instead. Up and Down recall what was sent.

"a" sends to each of your devices in a region instead: it asks for the
region, then the command, and sends each of your devices there its own command, one after another in random order, spaced
10-90 s apart (5-30 min for a restart); there is no broadcast. A node on
lou-20 or later gets a sealed command on its own topic, one on lou-19
or older the old per-node scheme. A command that transmits on the air
(a "trigger") cannot be sent to a region; send it to one device.

Tagged devices in two different regions cannot be sent to at once: a send
is to one region.

Commands that take something away ask first; see [[cautions]]. For what a
repeater admits, see [[commands]]. A refusal is explained in words:
"refused: not-allowed" means the firmware does not admit that command;
"rate-limited" means wait a few minutes; "sig-invalid" means the node
holds a different public key than yours (see [[signing]]).
