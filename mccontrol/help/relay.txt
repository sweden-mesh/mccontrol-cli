Through a relay

From lou-20 a node of yours that is on MQTT can send a command by radio
to a node that is not: a mesh-only node (see [[meshonly]]), or one whose
Wi-Fi or broker has failed (see [[mesh]]). You need no companion radio.
The node that sends is the relay; the node the command is for is the
target.

  mccontrol send cabin ver --via relay:loutest

What can be a relay
  An Observer image with remote control (rc or arrc) on lou-20 or later,
  on MQTT, holding the SAME command key as the target, and in radio
  reach of it, directly or over the mesh. A relay serves the holder of
  its own command key and nobody else. A node without a command key
  never relays, and a mesh-only node cannot: it has no MQTT.

What the relay does
  It sends the mesh request the tool built for the target, waits up to
  two minutes, and returns the target's answer over MQTT. It runs
  nothing itself and cannot read the request or the answer: both are
  sealed between the target and you.

The route
  direct      one transmission, no hops: right when the target hears
              the relay itself. The default.
  --path A1B2 direct along the hops you give ("2:A1B2C3D4" for two-byte
              hashes, "--path none" for no hops again).
  --route flood       a flood in the relay's region home.
  --route flood-wide  a flood in the relay's default scope, which is
                      wider.
  An answer teaches the path. The next direct send through that relay
  uses it. A flood costs every repeater that hears it airtime, so flood
  once to learn the way, then send direct.

One command at a time
  A relay holds one command. A second one that arrives before the first
  is on the air replaces it, and the first is never answered. Send one
  and wait for its answer. One node at a time: never a region, a county
  or a group.

The relay's budget
  Airtime: 5 % of 15 minutes (45 s on the air, about 26 commands), then
  1 % of the next 15 minutes (9 s, about 5), then a burst again.
  Floods: one a minute in a burst, one in five minutes after it, 100 a
  day, and two an hour for floods that leave the relay's region home.
  The budget is fixed. A restart of the relay opens a new burst.
  "get relay" sent to the relay shows what is left:
    > own, forwarded 3, answered 2, refused 1, burst, 39.9 s airtime
    left, floods today 1 of 100

What comes back
  The target's answer, with how well the relay heard it. Or one word
  from the relay:
    no answer     sent, nothing came back. Not a refusal: the target
                  may have run the command. See [[mesh]], "No answer".
    rate-limited  the relay's budget is used up. Wait a few minutes.
    busy          another command was waiting on the relay.
    off           relaying is switched off on the relay.
    clock-unset   the relay has no clock yet.
    expired, replay, bad-body, send-failed   nothing was sent.
  With every word but "no answer", nothing went on the air.

Switch relaying
  It is on by default. "set relay off" stops a node relaying, "set
  relay own" turns it on again. Both travel over the plane, so no visit
  is needed.

Who picks the relay
  On the command line you name the relay; --via auto never takes one.
  The web app's Auto may take a relay that hears the target directly,
  when no companion is connected, and never by a flood.

History keeps the relay, the route, how the command was sent and how
well the relay heard the answer (see [[history]]).
